TAPE

Analog Tape Machine

Controls

  • Drive0 to 24 dB — Input level into the tape saturation (level-matched at -12 dBFS, so it adds colour rather than volume)
  • Speed7.5 ips / 15 ips / 30 ips — Tape speed: sets the head bump frequency (45 / 70 / 110 Hz), the high-frequency loss and the emphasis corner
  • Head Bump0 to 9 dB — Height of the low-frequency head bump
  • Wow0 to 100% — Slow pitch drift of the transport (up to 2 ms delay modulation)
  • Flutter0 to 100% — Fast pitch wobble of the transport (up to 0.1 ms delay modulation)
  • Hiss0 to 100% — Tape hiss level, shaped by the tape speed
  • Output−12 to +12 dB — Output level
  • Mix0 to 100% — Dry/wet blend (the dry signal is delayed to stay aligned with the wet path)
  • Tape TypeClassic / Type I / Type II / Type IV — Tape formulation: Classic (open reel), Type I (ferric, warm, more hiss), Type II (chrome, more high-frequency headroom, brighter and quieter hiss) or Type IV (metal, cleanest and most extended)
  • Noise ReductionOff / On — Cassette-style noise reduction: a compander that boosts quiet highs up to 10 dB before the tape and cuts them after it, lowering the hiss with a slight breathing on loud highs

Best uses

  • Glue and warmth on the mix bus
  • Rounder low end and softer transients on drums and bass
  • Lo-fi, cassette-like wobble with Wow and Flutter turned up
  • Subtle analog colour on vocals and acoustic tracks

Properties

Signal chain: Input → Wow/Flutter Modulated Delay (3 ms centre, cubic interpolation) → Pre-Emphasis → Drive → Asymmetric Saturation with memory (hysteresis-like) → De-Emphasis → Low Cut + Head Bump → Hiss → High-Frequency Loss → Mix → Output. The hiss is an exact Park-Miller generator. The dry signal is delayed by the same 3 ms, and the 3 ms latency is reported to the host for delay compensation.
