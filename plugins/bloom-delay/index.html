BLOOM DELAY BETA

Delay That Blooms Into Reverb

Controls

  • Time20 to 2000 ms — Delay time in milliseconds, used while Sync is Off
  • SyncOff / 1/16 / 1/8 / 1/8 Dotted / 1/4 / 1/4 Dotted / 1/2 / 1/1 — Locks the delay to the project tempo: 1/16 to a whole note, dotted 1/8 and 1/4 too (120 bpm when the host gives no tempo)
  • Feedback0 to 100% — How much of each repeat comes back; a soft curve in the loop keeps even 100% safe, for a near-endless wash
  • Bloom0 to 100% — How much of the allpass diffusion is blended into the loop: each repeat comes back softer and more smeared, until the echoes turn into a reverb
  • Mod0 to 100% — A slow sweep of the delay time (0.35 Hz, up to 4 ms) for a gentle, tape-like movement
  • Damping1000 to 20000 Hz — Low-pass in the loop: lower values make every repeat darker
  • Width0 to 100% — Stereo width of the wet signal, from mono to full
  • Mix0 to 100% — Blend with the dry signal
  • Output−24 to +12 dB — Output level

Best uses

  • Ambient guitars and keys: echoes that dissolve into a wash
  • Vocal throws that bloom instead of repeating
  • Endless pads and drones with Feedback at 100%
  • Tempo-synced dotted eighths with a reverb tail
  • A small diffuse space with a short Time and full Bloom

Properties

Input + feedback → 4 s stereo delay line read with linear interpolation (Time or Sync from the tempo, 80 ms glide, swept by a 0.35 Hz LFO with the sides a quarter turn apart) → one-pole low-pass (Damping) → 30 Hz high-pass → four allpass diffusers per side (g 0.6, 3.6 to 12.7 ms), blended in by Bloom → feedback through a tanh curve back into the line. The wet signal is narrowed by Width and mixed with the dry signal → Output gain. No latency.
